# Design Thinking Workshop 🎨

Human-Centered Problem Solving

*A practical guide to the design thinking process*

---

## What is Design Thinking?

A human-centered approach to innovation that draws from the designer's toolkit.

**Core Principles:**
- 🧑 Human-centered
- 🔄 Iterative process
- 🤝 Collaborative
- 🎯 Action-oriented
- 🧪 Experimental mindset

---

## Why Design Thinking?

Traditional problem-solving often fails because:
- Solutions based on assumptions
- Users not involved in process
- Focus on features, not problems
- Linear approach misses insights

**Design thinking puts users first!**

---

## The Five Phases

```
1. EMPATHIZE
   ↓
2. DEFINE
   ↓
3. IDEATE
   ↓
4. PROTOTYPE
   ↓
5. TEST
   ↓
(iterate)
```

Non-linear! Jump between phases as needed.

---

## Phase 1: Empathize 🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Understand the people you're designing for

**Methods:**
- User interviews
- Observation
- Immersion
- Engage with users
- Experience their environment

**Goal:** Gain empathy and insights

---

## Empathy Techniques

### User Interviews
- Ask "why?" five times
- Open-ended questions
- Listen more than talk
- Observe body language

### Observation
- Watch users in natural environment
- Note workarounds and pain points
- Capture emotions and reactions

---

## Empathy Map Template

```
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│   SAYS          │    THINKS       │
│  What do they   │   What are they │
│  say out loud?  │   thinking?     │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│   DOES          │    FEELS        │
│  What actions   │   Emotional     │
│  do they take?  │   state?        │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

Document user perspectives!

---

## Phase 2: Define 🎯

Synthesize insights into actionable problem statement

**Process:**
1. Organize research findings
2. Identify patterns
3. Find insights
4. Define problem clearly

**Goal:** Clear point of view (POV)

---

## Creating a POV Statement

**Format:**
```
[User] needs [need]
because [insight]
```

**Example:**
```
Busy parents need quick, healthy 
meal options because they lack time 
to cook after work but want to feed 
their family nutritious food.
```

Make it specific and actionable!

---

## How Might We (HMW) Questions

Transform POV into opportunities

**Examples:**
- HMW make meal prep faster?
- HMW make healthy food more convenient?
- HMW reduce decision fatigue?
- HMW involve kids in meal planning?

Generative, not restrictive!

---

## Phase 3: Ideate 💡

Generate a wide range of ideas

**Rules:**
1. Defer judgment
2. Encourage wild ideas
3. Build on others' ideas
4. Stay focused on topic
5. One conversation at a time
6. Be visual
7. Go for quantity

---

## Ideation Techniques

### Brainstorming
- Set time limit (15-30 min)
- Post-its on wall
- Everyone participates
- No idea too crazy

### Crazy 8s
- Fold paper into 8 sections
- 1 minute per idea
- Sketch concepts
- Forces rapid ideation

---

## Ideation: Mind Mapping

```
         ┌─── Feature A
         │
    ┌────┴─── Feature B
    │    │
    │    └─── Feature C
PROBLEM──┤
    │    ┌─── Approach 1
    │    │
    └────┴─── Approach 2
         │
         └─── Approach 3
```

Explore connections and possibilities!

---

## Converging on Ideas

After generating many ideas:

**Dot Voting:**
- Each person gets 3-5 dots
- Vote for favorite ideas
- Tally results
- Discuss top ideas

**Prioritization Matrix:**
- Impact vs Effort
- Focus on high impact, low effort

---

## Phase 4: Prototype 🛠️

Build quick, low-fidelity representations

**Remember:**
- Fast and cheap
- Focus on testing assumptions
- Multiple iterations expected
- Fail fast, learn faster

**Types:** Paper, digital, physical, role-play

---

## Prototyping Levels

**Low Fidelity:**
- Paper sketches
- Storyboards
- Cardboard models
- Wireframes

**Medium Fidelity:**
- Clickable mockups
- 3D prints
- Video scenarios

**High Fidelity:**
- Working prototypes
- Interactive demos

---

## Prototype Best Practices

✅ **Start low-fi** - Don't get attached
✅ **Test assumptions** - Focus on learning
✅ **Build just enough** - Minimum viable
✅ **Get feedback early** - Iterate quickly
✅ **Embrace failure** - Each failure teaches

---

## Phase 5: Test 🧪

Gather feedback and refine solutions

**Process:**
1. Show prototype to users
2. Observe their interaction
3. Ask questions
4. Document feedback
5. Iterate based on learnings

**Goal:** Learn and improve

---

## Testing Guidelines

**Do:**
- Let users interact naturally
- Observe without interfering
- Ask open-ended questions
- Note unexpected behaviors
- Test with diverse users

**Don't:**
- Lead or guide users
- Defend your design
- Take feedback personally
- Test only with friends

---

## Test Feedback Framework

**What to capture:**

📝 **Observations:**
- What did users do?
- Where did they struggle?
- What surprised them?

💬 **Quotes:**
- Record exact words
- Capture emotions
- Note suggestions

🎯 **Insights:**
- What did we learn?
- What needs to change?

---

## Iterating After Testing

```
Test Results
    ↓
Identify Issues
    ↓
┌───┴───┐
│       │
Minor   Major
Change  Change
│       │
Update  Return to
Prototype  Earlier Phase
```

Iterate based on findings!

## Real-World Example: Shopping Cart

**Empathize:**
- Observed customers abandoning carts
- Interviewed frustrated shoppers

**Define:**
- Users need confidence in purchase decisions

**Ideate:**
- AR try-before-buy
- Detailed product videos
- Customer reviews
- Easy returns

---

## Shopping Cart (continued)

**Prototype:**
- Mockup of AR feature
- Video demo interface
- Enhanced review section

**Test:**
- Users loved AR concept
- Wanted size comparisons
- Needed better filters

**Iterate:** Refined based on feedback!

❌ **Not involving users** enough
❌ **Falling in love** with first idea
❌ **Making assumptions** without testing
❌ **Giving up** after first failure
❌ **Working in isolation**

## Key Principles Recap

1. **Human-Centered**: Start with people
2. **Show Don't Tell**: Make ideas tangible
3. **Radical Collaboration**: Diverse teams
4. **Embrace Experimentation**: Try things
5. **Bias Toward Action**: Do, don't just think
6. **Mindful of Process**: Trust the method

## When to Use Design Thinking

**Great for:**
- Complex problems
- Unknown solutions
- User-facing products
- Innovation challenges
- Culture change

**Less suitable for:**
- Simple, well-defined problems
- Technical optimization
- Time-critical fixes
